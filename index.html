The name game

✳ Anthropic Greek for “human being”

From the Greek ánthrōpos — human being. The name gestures at the anthropic principle, the idea that any theory of the universe has to account for the fact that observers exist inside it to ask the question.

Founded in 2021 by siblings Dario Amodei (VP of Research at OpenAI) and Daniela Amodei (VP of Safety & Policy), who left along with a group of colleagues including Jared Kaplan, Tom Brown, Chris Olah, Sam McCandlish, Jack Clark and Benjamin Mann.

And Claude? Named for Claude Shannon, the father of information theory, who in 1948 worked out that information could be measured in bits — the idea nearly all of modern computing rests on. He grew up in Gaylord, Michigan, population about 4,000.

Founded 2021 · San Francisco

◍ OpenAI The “open” was a promise

Founded in December 2015 as a non-profit, with the name announcing the plan: AI research published openly, as a counterweight to the same work happening behind closed doors at large companies.

The structure changed as training costs climbed into the billions, and the name has been a running argument ever since — which is what happens when you put a commitment in your company name.

ChatGPT was launched in November 2022 as a low-key “research preview.” It became the fastest-growing consumer product in history, which nobody involved had planned for.

Founded 2015 · San Francisco

◈ Google DeepMind Deep learning + neuroscience

Founded in London in 2010 by Demis Hassabis, Shane Legg and Mustafa Suleyman. The name welds deep learning to the mind — the founding bet was that neuroscience and AI should be studied together.

Google acquired it in 2014 for a reported $500 million. In April 2023 it merged with Google Brain to become Google DeepMind.

Hassabis was a chess prodigy who reached master level at 13, then went into video games — he was a lead designer on the 1994 hit Theme Park while still a teenager. He was knighted in 2024 and shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry the same year for AlphaFold.

Founded 2010 · London

♊ Gemini The twins

Named for the constellation of the twins — fitting for a model built by two merged research teams, Google Brain and DeepMind, that had spent a decade as rivals inside the same company.

There is a second reading the team has nodded at: Project Gemini, NASA’s two-seat programme of the mid-1960s. Gemini was the unglamorous middle child between Mercury and Apollo — the one that proved rendezvous and docking, without which the Moon landing could not have happened.

Announced 2023 · Mountain View

▲ NVIDIA Latin for envy

From invidia, Latin for envy — the founders wanted graphics that the rest of the industry would covet. It blends with “NV”, short for “next version,” which is what they had been naming their files.

Founded on 25 January 1993 by Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem — over coffee in a booth at a Denny’s in San Jose. Huang had been a dishwasher and busboy at Denny’s as a teenager. The booth now has a plaque.

The company that sold graphics cards to gamers turned out to own the hardware the entire AI industry runs on. In September 2026 it agreed to buy Hugging Face for about $13 billion — its second-largest acquisition ever.

Founded 1993 · San Jose

🤗 Hugging Face Named after an emoji

Straightforwardly named after 🤗, the hugging face emoji. Founded in 2016 in New York by three French entrepreneurs who were building a chatbot for bored teenagers.

The chatbot went nowhere. The tooling they had built to make it work turned into the default place the world shares machine-learning models — over 3 million of them, used by more than 18 million developers.

In September 2026, NVIDIA agreed to acquire it for roughly $13 billion. Which means an emoji a teenage-chatbot startup picked in 2016 is now worth about as much as a mid-size airline.

Founded 2016 · New York

≋ Mistral AI A cold wind in Provence

The mistral is a strong, cold, dry north-westerly that funnels down the Rhône valley into the Mediterranean. It can blow for days, and it is famous for scouring the sky to a hard clear blue.

Founded in Paris in 2023 by Arthur Mensch (ex-DeepMind), Guillaume Lample and Timothée Lacroix (both ex-Meta), who had met as students at École Polytechnique.

A very deliberate piece of naming: something unmistakably French and regional, from a company whose pitch was that Europe should not depend on American labs for its AI.

Founded 2023 · Paris

✕ Grok · xAI A word invented in 1961

Grok was coined by Robert A. Heinlein in his 1961 novel Stranger in a Strange Land. In the book it is a Martian word, and it means to understand something so completely that you merge with it — comprehension as absorption, not as analysis.

It escaped the novel into 1960s counterculture, then into programmer slang, and is now in the Oxford English Dictionary: “to understand intuitively or by empathy.”

Heinlein’s Martians reportedly considered the word untranslatable, on the grounds that you would have to already grok it to understand the explanation.

Founded 2023 · Bay Area

◎ Cohere Text that hangs together

To cohere is to hold together — which is exactly the thing language models are graded on. Founded in Toronto in 2019.

Co-founder Aidan Gomez was one of the eight authors of Attention Is All You Need, the 2017 Google paper that introduced the Transformer. He was an intern at the time. Nearly every author of that paper has since left to found or join an AI company.

Founded 2019 · Toronto

? Perplexity Borrowed from the scoreboard

Perplexity is the classic measure of how good a language model is: roughly, how surprised the model is by text it has not seen. Low perplexity means the next word was close to what it expected.

An in-joke of a name for a search company — naming yourself after the thing you are trying to drive down.

One way to picture it: a perplexity of 20 means the model is about as unsure as if it were picking uniformly between 20 equally plausible next words.

Founded 2022 · San Francisco

✉ ELIZA From a Bernard Shaw play

The first chatbot, written by Joseph Weizenbaum at MIT between 1964 and 1966. Named after Eliza Doolittle in George Bernard Shaw’s Pygmalion — the flower girl taught to speak like a duchess without changing who she is. A pointed choice: the program imitated understanding without having any.

Its best-known script, DOCTOR, parodied a psychotherapist by turning your statements back into questions. It was about 200 lines of pattern-matching.

People confided in it anyway — reportedly including Weizenbaum’s own secretary, who asked him to leave the room. He spent much of the rest of his life warning about what he had found, and the effect is still called the ELIZA effect.

1966 · MIT

⌁ The Transformer Titled after a Beatles song

The architecture under the “T” in GPT arrived in a 2017 Google paper by eight authors: Attention Is All You Need. The title is a play on the Beatles’ “All You Need Is Love.”

Co-author Llion Jones has said the title took him about five seconds to think of, and that he did not expect the others to go for it.

The claim in the title was technical and slightly cheeky: you could throw out the recurrence and convolutions everyone assumed were essential, and keep only the attention mechanism. It worked, and it is why any of the rest of this page exists.

2017 · Google

How it got here

Seventy years, compressed.

  1. 1948

    Information becomes measurable

    Claude Shannon publishes A Mathematical Theory of Communication and gives the world the bit. Everything downstream depends on it.

  2. 1956

    The field gets its name

    John McCarthy coins “artificial intelligence” in the proposal for a summer workshop at Dartmouth. He reportedly picked it partly to avoid arguing about whether it counted as cybernetics.

  3. 1966

    The first chatbot

    ELIZA convinces people it understands them using roughly 200 lines of pattern matching. Its author is alarmed rather than delighted.

  4. 1993

    Three engineers, one Denny’s

    Nvidia is founded in a roadside diner booth in San Jose, to make graphics chips for video games.

  5. 2010

    DeepMind

    A London startup bets that neuroscience is the route to general intelligence. Google buys it four years later.

  6. 2017

    Attention is all you need

    Eight authors at Google publish the Transformer. Almost all of them eventually leave to start companies.

  7. 2021

    Anthropic

    A group of OpenAI researchers leave to build a lab organised around safety, and name its assistant after Shannon.

  8. 2022

    The research preview

    ChatGPT ships in November and the general public meets a language model for the first time.

  9. 2026

    The emoji gets acquired

    Nvidia — the envy company from the diner — agrees to buy Hugging Face, the emoji company from the teenage chatbot, for about $13 billion.
